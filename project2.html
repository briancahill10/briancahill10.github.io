Ultrasonic Transducer Mount

SolidWorks & Rapid Prototyping

Problem

AcousticaBio purchased a new transducer and the dimensions and weight were larger than the previous transducer. Because of this, we needed to remake the mount to accommodate the changes.

Action

First I drafted a design in SolidWorks. Then I adjusted that design to accommodate space constraints, ease of set up, and the weight of the transducer. I then 3D printed an initial version before identifying necessary changes and making adjustments in a subsequent iteration. Finally, I reprinted the piece and implemented it on the printer.

Outcome

A sturdy 3D printed mount that supports the transducer, prevents deformation over time, and allows for easy set up.

Acoustophoretic Printhead

Mechanical Design & SolidWorks

Problem

The new transducer emitted sound waves at a different frequency than the previous one. Because of this, the parameters of the printhead that channeled the acoustic field had to be adjusted.

Action

  • –

    On the previous version of the printer the back reflector was adjusted manually. In a system where precision was imperative this didn't seem logical, so I designed, built, and implemented a back reflector bridge that held a motor and could be moved from the desktop control system. The bridge also acted as a brace for the acoustic chamber.

  • –

    The acoustic chamber itself was adjusted to accommodate the new transducer. The new transducer required a wider and longer channel in the center. I calculated these dimensions using equations found in a paper that depended on the wavelength and in turn the frequency of the transducer.

  • –

    I designed, printed, and implemented printhead snaps so they could easily snap fit into the chamber depending on the transducer being used — saving a significant amount of time during testing.

Outcome

The new printhead design was able to produce an acoustophoretic force of about 250g — 25% higher than the previous version. It was also easier for the user to operate because of the automation of the back reflector.

Laser Cut Acrylic Electronics Panels

Laser Cutting & Design

Problem

In the second iteration of our printer the electronics changed, so we needed a new design of acrylic panels for the electronics cart.

Action

Designed acrylic panels to be laser cut and fit all of the necessary pieces.

Outcome

3 functional shelves of electronics nicely spaced and put together on the electronics and hardware cart to keep components labeled and organized.
