Acoustophoretic Force Analysis

System Analysis & Data Analysis

Python ImageJ (IJM) Data Analysis System Analysis AcousticaBio

Problem

My team wanted to measure the force that our acoustophoretic printer was producing at the droplet's point of detachment from the nozzle. To directly measure this, we would have needed a microphone inside our 1.5mm cylindrical chamber. This was not possible because:

  • –

    The microphone would affect the acoustic field, giving incorrect readings.

  • –

    Having a microphone directly below the nozzle would prevent any droplet from forming at all.

  • –

    The 1.5mm diameter constraint made putting a microphone in the cylinder impractical.

An image of where we wanted to measure the acoustophoretic force.

Action

  • –

    We already had a camera set up, so I curated camera presets to take videos of the droplets right below the nozzle (because then we know they would be fully formed) and used this as a way to later calibrate pixels to microns.

  • –

    Then I used IJM to create a macro that analyzed the videos and created a CSV file of the areas of the droplets on screen. The volume is dependent on the acoustophoretic force, so I was able to calculate the force our printer was producing from that.

  • –

    Finally, I wrote a script in Python that translated the droplets in the CSV files from pixels to microns and produced statistics regarding the average force and the covariance of the force for all of the droplets.

Desktop showing a blown up version of a droplet on the nozzle tip.

Snippet of the IJM macro used to analyze videos of droplets from the printer.

Outcome

I produced a reliable and easy way to measure the acoustic force our printer was producing without affecting the acoustic field, and was able to efficiently produce charts and statistics on performance using my Python script and IJM macro.
